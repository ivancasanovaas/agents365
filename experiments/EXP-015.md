# EXP-015: dead-ball states that remember who had the ball (who restarts play)

STATUS: defined 2026-11-12 BEFORE running. Rule fixed. Same protocol as EXP-010/013 (7 development games, leave-one-game-out,
bench v2, 3 test seeds per game, temp 0.3, 32 base features). Reference: production MLP emissions with the 3-state HMM.
The 4 hold-out games are retired and are NOT used.

## Why (idea from the PathCRF paper, Kim et al. 2026, KDD '26)
PathCRF models the ball leaving the pitch as dedicated "outside" states and constrains which transitions are possible, instead of
letting every state reach every other with a fixed probability. In our 3-state HMM the dead-ball state D forgets who had the ball
before it: P(D -> H) and P(D -> A) are the same whatever happened before. But who restarts play depends on who had the ball
(ball out off the possessing team's touch -> the opponent restarts; a foul against the possessing team keeps it; a goal -> the
conceding team kicks off). There are about 130 stoppages per match, and each starts a new possession whose team is decided at the
moment when the evidence is weakest (players standing, ball often not yet visible). If the model remembers the previous
possessor, the restart team gets a learned prior.

## Candidate
Split D into two states, D_H (dead ball after a home possession) and D_A (after an away possession): 4 states H, A, D_H, D_A.
- Emissions: the same 3-class emission model; D_H and D_A both use the D emission (nothing about the evidence changes).
- Transitions: learned by counting on the training games' labels converted to 4 states (a D frame takes the team of the last H/A
  frame before it inside the same half; D frames before the first possession of a half are excluded from the counts), Laplace
  alpha 0.5, as for the 3-state matrix. Initial prior from the same label frequencies.
- Decoding: Viterbi over the 4 states inside the graded halves; D_H and D_A are written as `D`. Output labels are still H/A/D.
No other change (same MLP, same temperature 0.3, same forced-D block before the second-half kickoff).

## Decision rule (candidate vs the 3-state model measured in the same run)
Adopt iff: mean spellF1 gain over the 7 held-out games > 0.04; gain > 0 in at least 5 of 7 games; mean D-F1 and mean macroF1 drop
<= 0.02. Reported but not decisive: number of H/A runs, predicted D share.

## Not allowed
Using the competition match, its public GT or its output; adding further states or constraints after seeing results.

## Results (executed 2026-11-12, 7 development games, 3 seeds, bench v2)
Mean spellF1 / macroF1 / D-F1: 3-state 0.543/0.822/0.844 | 4-state 0.543/0.822/0.844. Per game the two are identical to three
decimals (e.g. skillcorner_1886347 0.583 vs 0.583; 349 vs 349 segments in metrica_game3).
4-state vs 3-state: mean dSpell **-0.000** (positive in 2/7), dD-F1 -0.000, dMacro -0.000 -> **fail. Decision: KEEP the 3-state model.**

Why (checked afterwards on the labels, aggregates only): in the external labels, the team that restarts after a dead ball is the
SAME team as the one that had the ball before in 327 stoppages and the OPPONENT in 288 (47% opponent). The memory carries no
information in these labels, so the learned D_H -> A / D_A -> H transitions are close to a coin flip and cannot move the decoding.
The football intuition "the opponent restarts" holds for balls put out by the possessing team, but is cancelled by fouls against it,
goals and other cases. A negative result, not a bug: the 4-state decoder is unit-tested (tests/test_hmm4.py) and does use a memory
when the training labels contain one.
